Field Architecture

Why a single dry-ridge drone fixes canyon blackout.

Solving 3D position and receiver clock bias requires four independent ranging equations. When mountain walls drop visible NavIC satellites to three, the geometry matrix collapses.

01
SAR Flood Masking

EOS-04 C-band radar penetrates storm clouds to separate flooded channels from verified 0.00 m dry ridges.

02
Dry-Ridge Tethered Relay

Our optimizer ranks every dry crest and launches a power-tethered drone pseudolite at 10–150 m altitude.

03
Full-Rank Geometry Recovery

The drone supplies the 4th independent row in the geometry matrix, restoring sub-5m fixes across rescue corridors.

Standing on peer-reviewed research papers and fixing where they broke.

NavIC-Restore does not claim to invent navigation physics from thin air. We systematically audited the fatal real-world failure points in landmark IEEE, Stanford, and Remote Sensing research papers, then engineered the missing hardware and mathematical bridges for mountain flash floods.

ISRO-NDRF ARCHIVAL SLEEVE 01 RESEARCH PAPER 01
RESEARCH PAPER 01 · UAV PSEUDOLITE IEEE (2006) · Stanford (1997)

Airborne Pseudolite for Disaster Recovery Positioning

T. Tsujii, M. Harigae (JAXA) · H. S. Cobb (Stanford Univ.)

LIMITATION: 20-MIN BATTERY SHUTDOWN
Landmark Research Publication
IEEE Aerospace Conference (2006) / Stanford Ph.D. Dissertation (1997)
Pioneering Contribution

Demonstrated that airborne transmitters (pseudolites) mounted on free-flying aircraft can broadcast synthetic GNSS ranging signals into GPS shadow zones.

⚠ THE FATAL MOUNTAIN LIMITATION WE FIXED:

Free-flying multicopters exhaust batteries in 20–25 minutes, rendering them useless for 72-hour disaster relief. Furthermore, unanchored free flight introduces severe transmitter position uncertainty (±3–5m) and oscillator clock bias drift (Δt_p), which directly pollutes user pseudo-range calculations on the ground.

✓ NAVIC-RESTORE ARCHITECTURAL INNOVATION:

Tethered Crest Pseudolite (TCP) with Ground OCXO Discipline: We replaced free flight with a micro-filament tether anchored to a SAR-verified ridge (55–150m AGL). Continuous ground power delivers unlimited 72+ hour mission endurance. High-frequency micro-coax hardwires transmitter synchronization directly to a ground-station Rubidium/OCXO reference clock (drift <1.2 ns), eliminating clock bias errors and locking geometric antenna coordinates.

High-Resolution SAR Flood Inundation Mapping in Basins

G. Schumann et al. (RSE) · S. Martinis et al. (DLR / IEEE JSTARS)

FATAL LIMITATION: PASSIVE DISCONNECTED GIS MAP
Landmark Research Publication
Remote Sensing of Environment (2007) / IEEE JSTARS (2015)
Pioneering Contribution

Developed automatic radiometric thresholding of satellite SAR backscatter (σ^0) to delineate standing floodwaters through dense clouds and heavy monsoon rain.

⚠ THE FATAL MOUNTAIN LIMITATION WE FIXED:

SAR radar was historically confined to passive, post-event GIS photography, showing where water flooded hours after the disaster. It was completely disconnected from real-time positioning systems, offering zero guidance on where rescue beacons should deploy.

✓ NAVIC-RESTORE ARCHITECTURAL INNOVATION:

In-the-Loop SAR-to-Geometry Autonomous Solver: NavIC-Restore transforms ISRO EOS-04 C-band SAR backscatter (σ^0 < -18 dB specular reflection for water vs. dry rock) into a real-time constraint solver. The system filters candidate ridges for verified dry ground (0.00m water) and autonomously maximizes det(G_augᵀ G_aug). This guarantees the tethered relay deploys onto the mathematically optimal crest minimizing corridor GDOP before takeoff.

ISRO-NDRF ARCHIVAL SLEEVE 03 RESEARCH PAPER 03
RESEARCH PAPER 03 · BARO-DEM AIDING IEEE PLANS (1993) · Artech House (2013)

GPS RAIM with Barometric Altimeter Aiding

R. G. Brown (IEEE PLANS) · P. D. Groves (Univ. College London)

LIMITATION: STORM DEPRESSION >150m ERROR
Landmark Research Publication
IEEE PLANS Conference Record (1993) / Principles of GNSS (Artech House, 2013)
Pioneering Contribution

Formulated the mathematical integration of barometric altimeters into the GNSS least-squares equation, replacing an occluded fourth satellite along the vertical z-axis.

⚠ THE FATAL MOUNTAIN LIMITATION WE FIXED:

Standard barometric aiding assumes a static standard sea-level atmosphere (1013.25 hPa). In Himalayan cloudbursts or monsoonal flash floods, local barometric depressions of 15–25 hPa cause standard baro-altimeters to overestimate altitude by over 150 meters, corrupting receiver position fixes.

✓ NAVIC-RESTORE ARCHITECTURAL INNOVATION:

Dual-Constraint Baro-DEM Riverbed Surface Clamp (Mode 03): When extreme canyon overhangs block even the drone relay, NavIC-Restore fuses receiver differential pressure (ΔP) with Cartosat-1 30m stereo DEM terrain surface contours. By projecting receiver altitude onto the known physical canyon riverbed floor, the system clamps the vertical axis and preserves 2D horizontal accuracy (±4.8m) even under severe 3-satellite blackout.
